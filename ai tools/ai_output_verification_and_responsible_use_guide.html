Executive Mandate

Generative Artificial Intelligence is an assistive ideation and drafting tool, not an authoritative knowledge base. Because Large Language Models (LLMs) operate on statistical probability rather than conscious fact retrieval, all generated text, code, and analytical claims require diligent human verification before publication, distribution, or code execution.

1 Why AI-Generated Responses Can Be Incorrect

AI models predict the most probable subsequent tokens based on pattern distributions within their training corpus. They do not maintain an understanding of factual validity, which leads to predictable failure modalities:

  • Hallucination: Models present fabricated assertions with high stylistic fluency and assertive confidence.
  • Knowledge Latency: Offline models possess static cutoff dates and cannot account for recent regulatory, commercial, or factual updates.
  • Contextual Fragility: Models lack genuine situational awareness and may misread subtle nuances, edge constraints, or user intent.

2 Fact-Checking Important Information

Never utilize AI-generated responses as the sole authoritative citation for decisions involving legal, medical, compliance, financial, or engineering domains.

  • Primary Source Tracing: Require verification against recognized primary repositories (e.g., official statutory texts, peer-reviewed journals, verified APIs).
  • Independent Cross-Referencing: Corroborate all statistical metrics, monetary figures, and chronological claims across at least two independent verified sources.
Rule of Thumb: If an incorrect claim could incur legal liability, brand harm, or technical disruption, automated AI output must never bypass manual expert review.

3 Identifying Fabricated References & Unsupported Claims

A recognized risk of modern LLMs is the synthetic generation of academic papers, hyperlinks, and judicial citations that appear genuine but are entirely non-existent.

Artifact Type Common AI Hallucination Verification Method
Hyperlinks & URLs Plausible URL syntax leading to HTTP 404 or hijacked domains. Inspect link targets directly in a secure browser sandbox before sharing.
Academic Citations Real author names paired with synthetic paper titles or false DOIs. Verify via Google Scholar, CrossRef, PubMed, or institutional library indexes.
Legal Precedents Invented case names, distorted rulings, or invalid docket numbers. Validate against official court registers, LexisNexis, or Westlaw archives.

4 Protecting Personal & Confidential Information

Information submitted to cloud-based public AI tools may be retained for retraining or review by third-party human evaluators, creating severe data breach vectors.

Strict Prohibition: Never input Personally Identifiable Information (PII), proprietary algorithms, credentials, financial ledgers, or non-public customer records into non-enterprise AI systems.
  • Data Masking & Sanitization: Replace identifiers, keys, and company names with dummy values prior to prompt submission.
  • Contractual Opt-Outs: Ensure enterprise vendor agreements legally guarantee zero retention for model training (Zero Data Retention / ZDR).

5 Copyright, Attribution, and Responsible Content Use

Generative systems may reproduce verbatim passages or stylistic likenesses from copyrighted training data, introducing copyright infringement and plagiarism exposure.

  • Substantive Transformation: AI outputs must serve solely as outlines or initial brainstorms; human authors must synthesize and refine final deliverables.
  • Transparent Disclosure: Adhere to organizational and publisher guidelines regarding transparent declaration of AI involvement in research and creative workflows.

6 Reviewing Generated Code Before Running It

AI-generated code frequently contains subtle vulnerabilities, hallucinated library dependencies, or inefficient memory patterns despite looking syntactically clean.

  • Security Scrutiny: Inspect generated scripts for obsolete dependencies, hardcoded paths, injection vulnerabilities, and broken access controls.
  • Package Hallucination: Attackers often register fake package names hallucinated by AI (slopsquatting). Confirm package legitimacy prior to npm install or pip install.
  • Isolated Execution: Always execute unverified code in isolated sandboxes or containers with automated unit tests prior to staging deployment.

7 Using AI as an Assistant, Not an Autopilot

The ultimate responsibility for any published output, strategic recommendation, or compiled codebase rests entirely with the human operator.

The Co-Pilot Mindset: Treat generative AI like a brilliant but junior intern—delegate heavy research drafting and structuring, but rigorously verify every assumption, calculation, and assertion.
